##Executive Summary

Generative AI reshapes the Australian labor market through three paths: task automation, worker capacity enhancement, and industrial restructuring. OECD data shows that at least 20% of Australian workers' jobs can significantly improve efficiency with the help of AI. ABS data in July 2026 shows that the national unemployment rate remains at 4.5%, and the overall employment market remains resilient, but the differentiation of the labor market gradually emerges: the employment growth rate of AI high-exposure occupations is only 5.6%, significantly lower than the growth level of 9.5% of low-exposure occupations, wage polarization, regional gaps, and vulnerable groups Employment risks continue to expand.

This proposal designs three independent policy options, evaluates them from the four dimensions of efficiency, fairness, feasibility and financial cost, and finally recommends a package of transformation plans for the implementation of the three policy combinations. The goal of the program is to release the productivity dividends brought by AI, alleviate the income inequality caused by technological change, and help the groups affected by AI smoothly complete their career transformation.

## Definition and background of the problem

The impact of AI on the labor market is not simply mass unemployment, but the restructuring of internal tasks. Conventional cognitive work is more likely to be replaced by AI; while creative, analytical and interpersonal collaborative work can amplify the productivity of workers with the help of AI. Mandala Partners predicts that by 2030, about 7.2 million people in Australia (50% of the total workforce) will face the job change brought about by AI.

There are obvious regional differences in the impact of AI: New South Wales has the highest exposure to AI in jobs and Tasmania has the lowest. The differences stem from the industrial structure of each state. In the absence of supporting public policies, AI will exacerbate the existing labor market inequality, and it will be difficult for administrative clerks, residents in remote areas, temporary workers and other groups to share the benefits of AI development. Relying on authoritative data in Australia such as DEWR, JSA and ABS, this proposal designs labor transformation policies adapted to the domestic institutional environment.
